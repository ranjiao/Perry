## What landed

`perry-task signoff-offer` builds the selection from Perry's own measurements;
`done` records what came back. Every offered item is kept, labelled
`Perry verified` or `restated — Perry did not verify this`, and dispositioned.
Name and date come from `git config user.name` and today. The signature block
goes into the journal under `## V5 sign-off` **in the same transaction as the
row**, and the structured record rides in the close event.

The load-bearing piece is `check_no_user_claim`, enforced **at the option
builder** rather than by review — deliverable 6 asked for a mechanical test and
got a mechanical gate. `--also` is exempt, correctly: those are the user's own
words, and refusing them for describing what the user did would be backwards.

## Three dispositions, not two — and the reasoning is better than the spec's

The spec's subjective line asked whether `accepted on report` was the right
second category or whether a third was needed. The agent implemented three, and
its first argument is the one that settles it: **the corpus already writes the
third by hand.** TASK-034's signature carries a section headed *"Not checked, and
recorded because V5's whole value is saying so"*; TASK-047's separates
*fixture opt-out 的理由已读并接受* — read, then accepted — from two costs taken
on the strength of Perry's printed output. A format that cannot hold what the
three existing signatures already say is a regression against its own corpus.

Its second argument is the drafting rule turned on itself: defaulting an
unselected item to `accepted on report` is already the outer edge of what Perry
may assert, defensible only because it describes the **scope of the signature**
rather than an act the user performed. Defaulting to `not looked at` would have
Perry assert something strictly stronger about a human. So `not looked at` is
**never a default** — reachable only by the user naming the item, which is what
keeps it a user statement rather than a Perry statement.

## A finding handed back to the PMO

The CJK half of the drafting guard initially never fired: `\w` matches CJK, so
`用户已阅` has no word boundary after `用户`. This is the **same defect class as
TASK-107** — an ASCII word-boundary assumption applied to Chinese — caught here
by the test corpus rather than in the field, and fixed by moving the CJK terms
outside the boundary assertions, with the reasoning in a code comment so it does
not regress.
