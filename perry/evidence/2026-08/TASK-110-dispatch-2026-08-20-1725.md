## The measurement is reproducible in the way that matters

Figures come from **git objects at a named commit**, not from the filesystem.
`du` measures whichever untracked scratch files happen to be lying around;
tracked blob sizes at a sha are the same for everyone with the same clone, and
the report prints the sha. Two consecutive runs at `bcf6771` are byte-identical.
The path list is read from `schema/state-schema.json § claims` and a test asserts
the tool contains no literal `"evidence/"`.

At `bcf6771`: Perry state is **1,864,239 bytes / 175 files / 33.3%** of tracked
bytes. `perry/evidence/` alone is 1,005,024 / 135 / 17.9%.

**It corrected the spec's own table**, which the PMO wrote: the `evidence/`
figures were the same measurement in KiB and the 36-file row was an earlier
commit quoted in decimal KB — two rows mixing units. The 31.3% share was a
different commit. At `bcf6771` the honest number is 33.3%.

## The number that decides it

Perry state grew **405,349 bytes/day** over 2026-08-16..20; `evidence/` alone
grew 240,831/day. The largest proposed rule recovers 560,089 bytes.

> **The entire proposal recovers 1.4 days of growth, once.**

Nothing in it is a policy that keeps pace. It is a one-off sweep that the next
dispatch campaign erases by Thursday. That sentence is the deliverable — a
measurement whose own conclusion is that the thing it was asked to propose is
not worth much.

## The obvious candidate is not where the bytes are

`.perry/events.jsonl` is documented as derived and disposable, so it is where a
reader would look first. It is **12% of `evidence/`** — and its history/bytes
ratio is **61×**, because the file is rewritten whole on every commit. Rotating
it recovers 225 KB of checkout and **none** of the 13.6 MB already spent. It is
simultaneously the largest single contributor to repository history and nearly
the cheapest thing to delete.

## Two findings it surfaced that outlive the retention question

1. **A handoff document's central measurement is stale.**
   `perry/handoff/2026-08-18-event-log-is-not-disposable.md` argued the log is
   not disposable, measured at `fed289c`: deleting it dropped 35 of 74 tasks
   from every contract payload. Re-measured at `bcf6771`, **that is no longer
   true** — `perry-task list --all` returns 106 records including all 80 `done`
   rows with or without the log, because TASK-089 put `perry/tasks.jsonl` in
   slot (1). The document's conclusion no longer follows from its own evidence.
2. **But deleting the log makes Perry report itself broken**, and two of its own
   tools then disagree: `perry-lint` declares **106 of 106 rows drifted**, every
   one on the `created` field, while `perry-tasks verify` reports
   `mismatch_count: 0` on the same tree. At least one is wrong. Out of scope
   there, and worth a row.

## The strongest argument in the document is against its own proposal

Handoffs are session-scoped, so an age-based rule is defensible — and the agent
proposed one *weakly*, then undercut it itself: the very document in finding 1,
`2026-08-18-event-log-is-not-disposable.md`, 4,752 bytes, **would have been
deleted by any age-based rule**, and it is what reopened the `.perry/` question.
Reporting the fact that kills your own rule is the behaviour verification 4 was
written to elicit.

## Verification 4 — "a reviewer can reach a different retention decision"

Met, emphatically. It lists **three grounds on which a reader can reject all of
it and be right**: the cost is not being paid where the bytes are (`perry-lint`
reads all 135 evidence files in **8 ms**); rule B's "unreferenced" selector
decays and rule A's deletes exactly the argument history a project that reviews
this hard presumably values; and deleting evidence retroactively weakens closes
that already happened — measured to break no *currently checked* guarantee, with
the caveat stated that "the check does not fire" is weaker than "the record is
intact".

## Where it says the real lever is, while declining to pull it

**65% of `evidence/` is review transcripts**, mean 13.9 KB, over four days that
were one dispatch campaign. So the lever is how much prose a V4 round is asked to
emit, not what gets deleted afterwards. It states this and then declines: *"it is
not this row's business, and it is a product decision, not a byte-counting one."*

## Two defects found in passing, neither in scope

- **`perry/.board.lock` is tracked in git.** A lock file. 0 bytes, correctness only.
- **The id minters read `max(board ∪ journal ∪ events)` and the store is not one
  of the three sources** (`mint_user_id`, `mint_cad_id`, `mint_risk_id`). Safe
  today only because the board carries the maximum in every family. It arms the
  moment a `USER-`/`RX-` row leaves the board while its journal record is gone:
  the next id is reissued and every prior reference silently re-points. Verified
  against `bin/perry-task:3174-3178`. Also: that file's own header still claims
  TASK ids come from those three sources; the implementation reads the store, so
  the docstring is stale.
